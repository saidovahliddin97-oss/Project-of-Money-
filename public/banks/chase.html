Convert Chase PDF statements to Excel, CSV or QuickBooks

Updated 2026-10-10

Need Chase transactions in a spreadsheet or your accounting software, but only have PDF statements? LocalLedger reads text-based Chase checking, savings and credit card statements inside your browser — the file is never uploaded — and rebuilds clean rows: date, description, signed amount and balance.

Step 1: Get the PDF from Chase

Sign in to Chase online banking, open the account and look for Statements (sometimes under Statements & documents or the account menu). Download each month as PDF. Statements downloaded this way contain real text, which is what a converter needs. A scan or a phone photo of a paper statement does not — run OCR on it first.

Only need the last few weeks? Online banking may offer a direct CSV or Excel download of recent activity — use it. PDF conversion is for older months and for statements someone sent you.

Step 2: Convert it

  1. Open the converter and drop one or more Chase PDFs on it (several months at once is fine).
  2. Check the summary: opening balance + transactions should equal the closing balance. A mismatch means a row was missed or misread.
  3. Review the table, fix anything odd, remove duplicates if months overlap.
  4. Download: QuickBooks Online, QuickBooks Desktop, Xero, Excel, CSV, QIF or OFX.

What is different about US statements

  • Dates: US statements usually use MM/DD (month first), often without the year on each row. LocalLedger detects the order from the whole file and fills in the year from the statement period.
  • Amounts: debits and credits are often in separate columns or marked with signs like CR/DR. You get one signed Amount column (money out negative) plus optional Money In / Money Out.
  • Credit cards: card statements print purchases as positive numbers. For your books they are money out, so LocalLedger detects card statements and flips the signs — payments to the card become positive.
  • Currency: amounts are kept as printed (USD); no conversion is applied.

Using QuickBooks Desktop? Convert to CSV here, then turn the CSV into an .IIF file with IIF Kit — rules like shell => Fuel map each row to your accounts.

Is it safe?

Most online converters upload your statement to their server. LocalLedger does the work with JavaScript on your own device, so the statement never leaves it. See is it safe to upload bank statements online?
